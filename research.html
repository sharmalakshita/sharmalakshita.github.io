Job Market Paper

Affirmative Action and Career Consequences: Evidence from India

This paper studies how increasing women’s representation in elite engineering programs affects their labor market outcomes along two margins: (a) first jobs at elite firms and (b) early-career promotions. I examine this question in the context of India’s 2018 Supernumerary Seats Scheme, a gender-based affirmative action policy that added female-only seats at the Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs) until women made up 20% of each program. Programs with fewer women before the reform added more seats and were more exposed to the policy. Using LinkedIn career histories and a novel classification of elite firms built with a large language model, I exploit this variation in exposure across programs in a difference-in-differences design. I document three main findings. First, in more exposed programs, the number of women entering the labor market roughly doubled, and elite firms hired about 60% of these additional women without hiring fewer men. Second, women in these programs were 21% more likely to begin their careers at an elite firm. Third, women from these programs who started at non-elite firms were more likely to be promoted within two years. These findings are consistent with latent demand for women among elite firms and with human capital accumulation via peer effects. Together, they suggest that gains from gender-based affirmative action can extend beyond education into the labor market.
